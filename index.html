Tactical urbanism · Singapore

Level Up

Turning the empty top deck of a Sengkang car park into a neighbourhood rooftop commons.

Development of Intelligent Cities · Term project
01 · Site
The site

The emptiest floor in a crowded town

Sengkang is one of Singapore's newest HDB towns. Its housing blocks share multi-storey car parks sized decades ago for car demand that never fully arrived.

  • The uncovered top deck is the hottest, furthest level — the last one anyone parks on, so it sits mostly empty.
  • New towns cluster shops and gathering places near transport hubs, leaving few informal places to meet near home.
  • Yet the deck is flat, built to carry load, reachable by lift and a few metres from thousands of residents — with views and an evening breeze.

02 · Intervention
The pilot

12 weekends. 4 zones. Nothing permanent.

"Level Up" opens the top deck on Friday–Sunday evenings and early mornings, when the heat eases. Everything is movable and cheap: shade sails, pallet seating, modular planters, string lights and paint.

04 · Participation & data
Built with residents

Residents design it, run it and vote on it

  • "Chalk the deck" day — residents draw the layout full-size on the concrete before anything is installed.
  • QR voting board at the lift lobby: anyone picks next weekend's film, class or market theme.
  • Deck captains — volunteers from residents' networks open, run and close each night.
  • Good-neighbour rules — 10 pm close, inward-facing speakers, a decibel limit.

05 · Path to permanence
From pop-up to policy

Test cheaply, then scale what works

Singapore already has the channels: the URA–HDB Lively Places Fund backs ground-up public-space ideas, and HDB has converted some car-park rooftops into community gardens and urban farms.

Week 0

Test night

One weekend, chalk-the-deck workshop, first sensor baseline.

Weeks 1–12

Pilot

Full programme, funded via Lively Places Fund and the town council.

Year 1

Make it permanent

If the data holds: a solar canopy for shade and power, fixed courts and garden beds, a residents' committee.

Year 2+

Playbook

An open guide for other under-used car-park roofs across the island.

06 · Impact
Expected impact

Dead concrete becomes a third place

Social

  • A walkable place to meet near home
  • Seniors, kids and neighbours share one space
  • Residents gain a say in their estate

Economic

  • Low-cost stalls for home-based businesses
  • Spending stays in the neighbourhood
  • No new land or construction needed

Functional & green

  • Single-use parking becomes multi-use space
  • Shade, planting and later solar cool the hottest surface
  • Growing supports Singapore Food Story 2
07
In one line

Low cost, gone in a day if it fails — and a model for hundreds of car-park roofs if it works.
