Tactical urbanism · Singapore

Level Up

Turning an empty car-park roof in Sengkang into a place where neighbours meet.

Development of Intelligent Cities · Term project
01 · Site
The site

Lots of people. One empty roof.

Sengkang is a new public-housing town in Singapore. Its car parks were built for more cars than people actually own.

  • The open roof is the hottest floor, so drivers avoid it and it stays almost empty.
  • Shops and cafés are far away near the train station, so there are few places to meet near home.
  • But the roof is flat, strong, has a lift, and is right next to thousands of homes — with a view and a cool evening breeze.

02 · Intervention
The pilot

12 weekends. 4 zones. Nothing permanent.

"Level Up" opens the roof on weekend evenings and mornings, when it is cooler. We use cheap things that are easy to remove: shade cloth, wooden seats, plant boxes, lights and paint.

Shade & Grow

Shade and plant boxes. Each family looks after one and grows herbs or vegetables.

04 · Participation & data
Built with residents

Made by the neighbours, for the neighbours

  • Chalk day — residents draw where things should go, in chalk, right on the roof.
  • QR-code vote by the lift: anyone can choose next weekend's film or activity.
  • Volunteer hosts — neighbours take turns to open and close each night.
  • Quiet rules — closes at 10 pm, speakers face away from homes, volume limit.
The smart-city part

Simple sensors count visitors, heat and noise, so the city can decide based on facts.

05 · Path to permanence
What happens next

Try it small. Keep what works.

Singapore already helps with this: a government fund (the Lively Places Fund) pays for ideas from residents, and some car-park roofs are already gardens and farms.

Week 0

Test night

One weekend to test. Residents plan the layout. Sensors start counting.

Weeks 1–12

Pilot

The full programme, paid for by the fund and the local town council.

Year 1

Make it permanent

If people keep coming: a solar roof for shade and power, fixed courts and gardens, run by residents.

Year 2+

Playbook

A free guide so other car-park roofs in Singapore can do the same.

06 · Impact
Expected impact

Unused concrete becomes a place to meet

Social

  • A place to meet, a short walk from home
  • Young and old spend time together
  • Residents have a say in their neighbourhood

Economic

  • Cheap stalls for small local sellers
  • Money is spent in the neighbourhood
  • No new land or buildings needed

Space & environment

  • A car park also becomes a park
  • Shade and plants make the hottest spot in the area cooler
  • Growing food supports Singapore's plan to grow more food locally
07
In one line

Cheap to try. Easy to remove. If it works, every car-park roof can do it.
